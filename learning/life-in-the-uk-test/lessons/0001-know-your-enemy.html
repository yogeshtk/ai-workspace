Lesson 1 · Orientation

Know Your Enemy: The Test & The Handbook

Before you memorise a single date, get the shape of what you're actually up against. Knowing the test's mechanics and the handbook's roadmap turns "study for the Life in the UK test" from a vague, anxious task into a concrete plan.

You're aiming to apply for settlement (ILR) in the next few months. The Life in the UK Test is a fixed, known quantity — it doesn't move, and it's drawn from exactly one book. That's good news: unlike an open-ended exam, you can know precisely what "fully prepared" looks like.

The test, stripped to its mechanics

ThingValue
Questions24, multiple choice
Time limit45 minutes
Pass mark18 / 24 (75%)
Fee£50 per sitting
BookingOnly via the official GOV.UK booking system, at least 3 days ahead

Two numbers worth sitting with: 45 minutes for 24 questions is roughly 110 seconds a question — generous if you know the material, tight if you're guessing. And 18/24 means you can get 6 wrong and still pass. That's not a reason to under-prepare — it's a reason not to panic over any single fact you're shaky on.

There is exactly one legitimate source

Every question on the test is drawn from one book: "Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents" (Home Office, 3rd edition). Not a summary of it, not a similar-sounding app, not "common knowledge" about Britain — that specific handbook, cover to cover.

This matters because the internet is full of Life in the UK Test apps and question banks of wildly varying quality. Treat all of them as rehearsal for recall you've already built from the handbook — never as a replacement for reading it.

The handbook's roadmap

Five chapters. This is your study plan, not just a table of contents:

ChapterCovers
1. Values & principlesDemocracy, rule of law, liberty, tolerance, participation
2. What is the UK?The four nations, geography, population, symbols
3. A long and illustrious historySettlement through to the modern era — dates, monarchs, wars, key figures
4. A modern, thriving societyCustoms, religion, arts, sport, everyday life
5. Government, the law, and your roleHow government works, devolved administrations, courts, voting, your responsibilities

Here's the strategic read on this, given where you're starting from: living in the UK day-to-day has probably already given you a real feel for chapters 2 and 4 — geography, culture, how things generally work. Chapters 3 and 5 are different — they're dense with specific facts, dates, and structural detail (which century, which monarch, how many members in which chamber) that you're much less likely to have absorbed just by living here. That's where your study time will earn the most marks per hour.

Tangible win You now know exactly what "prepared" means: 18/24 in 45 minutes, sourced entirely from one handbook, with chapters 3 and 5 as your highest-priority study targets.
